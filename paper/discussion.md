# Discussion (draft, 20 Sep 2026)

## What transfers, and what does not

The teacher-swap control answers the question in the title with unusual precision. Distillation at a conventional temperature does hand the student something specific to its teacher: the per-flow pattern of unknown-scores shifts measurably toward the teacher that produced the soft targets, and away from an equally accurate teacher that did not (H1). But that pattern is all that transfers. The teacher's *quality* as an unknown detector, its calibration and its robustness to shortcuts do not arrive with it. Against the cheapest possible control, the same student trained directly and temperature-scaled, distillation buys nothing on any unknown-detection outcome, and against label smoothing it trades detection for calibration. Inheriting the teacher's pattern even means inheriting its weaknesses: the T = 4 student takes on the teacher's poorer detection of near unknowns.

The temperature result reconciles two findings that would otherwise conflict. Tuned for accuracy, distillation chooses T = 1, at which a 96%-accurate teacher's targets are nearly one-hot and nothing teacher-specific survives; the student is then a directly trained student by another name. At T = 4 the teacher's signal comes through, at a cost of 1.7 macro-F1 points. Practitioners who tune distillation for accuracy, which is the norm in the traffic-classification literature we surveyed, are therefore not transferring the properties that distillation is often assumed to preserve, and practitioners who keep the textbook temperature are paying accuracy for a pattern that does not improve detection.

## The gap closes rather than opens

We expected the compressed student to age faster than its teacher. The opposite happened: over 35 weeks of real traffic the 11.3M-parameter ensemble lost unknown-detection AUROC faster than the 101k student, and from about the third month the student was the better detector. We offer two readings and cannot yet separate them. The ensemble's energy score aggregates five members' logit scales, which may drift more than a single small model's; and a higher-capacity model may fit more of the training period's transient structure, which then decays. Either way the deployment implication is the same and is the opposite of the usual worry: in this setting the small model is not the fragile one. The exception is label smoothing, whose gap to the teacher widened, so the effect is not a property of small models in general but of how they are trained.

Both teacher and students lose about 20 macro-F1 points over the year. Nothing in this study mitigates drift; it only shows that compression does not make it worse.

## Shortcuts: capacity, not pedagogy

The shortcut results separate two things that are easily conflated. Whether a student relies on a shortcut it can see is governed by its size: both 101k students leaned on the planted feature about twice as hard as the 2.3M teacher, regardless of whether they were distilled. This agrees with prior findings that compressed models bear a disproportionate share of a model family's brittleness [Hooker et al.], and it means that distillation is not a route to the teacher's shortcut robustness. What distillation *does* carry is confidence: a teacher that learned to lean on a shortcut produces, through its soft targets alone, a student that is more over-confident on data where the shortcut does not exist. That the same student detected unknowns slightly better is a reminder that calibration and open-set ranking are different quantities, and that improving one is no evidence about the other.

## Two results that run against the literature

**Label smoothing.** Several studies report that label smoothing degrades out-of-distribution detection by collapsing the geometry of the penultimate layer [refs: soft labels and OOD; label-smoothed embedding hypothesis; adaptive label smoothing; LS and selective classification]. In our grid the label-smoothed student is the second-best unknown detector with MSP and within 0.02 of the best with energy, ahead of every distilled student at T = 4 and of EnDD. We do not think the prior findings are wrong; we think the regime differs. Those studies use tens of classes, image features and a smoothing strength of 0.1; ours has 102 fine-grained services with heavy overlap, flow-level features, a tuned strength of 0.05, and unknowns that are themselves TLS services rather than a different modality. The degradation reported for label smoothing may depend on the OOD data being far from the training distribution, which near-unknown services are not. Reproducing the geometric analysis of those papers on this dataset would settle the point and is left for future work.

**Distribution distillation.** Ensemble distribution distillation and its descendants report students that match or exceed their teacher ensembles at uncertainty estimation [refs: logit-based EnDD; self-distribution distillation; credal ensemble distillation; innate uncertainty estimation]. Our EnDD student is the worst unknown detector in the grid with the energy score. Three differences from that literature matter. We used the proxy-Dirichlet objective rather than maximum-likelihood EnDD, because the latter was unstable with 102 classes in our pilot; the proxy target caps the precision at 10⁴, which makes the loss dominated by matching the total concentration, that is, the logit scale, rather than the members' disagreement; and our teacher ensemble's members disagree little (their mean energy AUROC is within 0.015 of the ensemble's), so there is little epistemic signal to distil. A negative result under these conditions should be read as "proxy EnDD did not preserve detection for a low-diversity ensemble on a 100-class task", not as a refutation of distribution distillation.

## Limitations

Novelty is simulated: no service first appears mid-year in CESNET-TLS-Year22, so unknown services are held out, and the near/far split is by the dataset's own category labels. The study is one dataset, one network, one year; the teacher family is a single architecture at two widths. The shortcut experiment plants a synthetic feature and runs on the validation week; natural shortcut fields (server IP, ASN, JA3) were excluded from the inputs rather than tested. H1's support rests more on `kdB4` than on `kdA4`, and the latter's null at start date 11 is unexplained. Baselines and the CPU deployment cost were measured on the validation week only. The pre-registration was frozen on validation data that had already informed eight design decisions, all documented; the test windows and their unknown services were not touched until the freeze, and the registration certifies the same document one day later.

## Conclusion

Asked what a distilled traffic classifier inherits, the answer is: its teacher's habits, not its teacher's abilities. The per-flow score pattern transfers, it transfers from the specific teacher used, and it carries that teacher's over-confidence and its blind spots along with it. What a network operator would actually want from distillation — unknown-traffic detection that holds up, calibration that can be trusted, robustness to shortcuts — a directly trained small model already has to the same degree, and keeps at least as well over a year of drift. If the goal is a small model that detects unknown traffic, this study finds no reason to train a large one first.
