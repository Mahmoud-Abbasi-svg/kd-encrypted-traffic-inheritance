Knowledge distillation is the standard way to compress encrypted-traffic classifiers for the network edge, and almost all of this work evaluates the student by accuracy alone. We ask what else the student inherits: its ability to detect unknown traffic, its calibration, its reliance on shortcut features, and whether any of this survives a year of drift. Resemblance to a teacher proves little on its own, because soft targets also act as a regulariser. We therefore distil the same 101k-parameter student from two teachers of equal accuracy but different construction (a five-member ensemble and a single wider model), so that behaviour following one teacher rather than the other is attributable to that teacher. The design was pre-registered publicly before any test result was computed. We tested ten hypotheses on CESNET-TLS-Year22, a year of real TLS traffic, across 18 test windows spanning 35 weeks after training. Two hypotheses are supported: a student's per-flow unknown-scores shift toward its own teacher, but only at a conventional temperature and not at the accuracy-optimal one; and a shortcut-reliant teacher passes its over-confidence to a student that never sees the shortcut. Reversed are the drift prediction, under both scores, since the teacher-student detection gap does not widen and under the energy score the student overtakes; and the prediction that a shortcut-reliant teacher degrades its student's unknown detection, which improves slightly instead. The rest are not supported, among them the prediction that distillation increases shortcut reliance, which is set by model size instead. Under the pre-registered logit-based scores nothing else transfers. Distillation beats neither a temperature-scaled direct student nor label smoothing. An exploratory analysis shows that this depends on the scoring rule. With a feature-space detector the teacher detects unknown traffic 0.073 AUROC better than the direct student, where the energy score sees 0.001, and the conventional-temperature student inherits most of that advantage. Label smoothing, which uses no teacher, recovers more of it still. Distillation transfers the teacher's habits; what looks like an inherited ability turns out to be available without a teacher.
